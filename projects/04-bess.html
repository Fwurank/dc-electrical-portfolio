Portfolio / Project 04

04 · BESS in a grid-constrained data center

bessdc: five studies of a 10 MW / 20 MWh BESS behind a 40 MW grid subscription: peak shaving, backup, revenue stacking, AI-load smoothing and the business case, with an interactive Streamlit tool.

Key findings

  • Peak shaving (LP, 8 760 h): without a BESS the campus is 2.08 MW over the limit on the design day. With the BESS there is room for +2.41 MW of extra IT, but only +0.84 MW counts as firm with one BESS unit out.
  • Backup: 27 min at full load. Replacing the gensets would take ≈ 790 MWh, about 5.5 × the genset cost. In island mode the PCS fault current (1.2 × In = 381 A at 20 kV) is not cleared by the grid-tuned relays, so the BESS is not counted as Tier III capacity.
  • Revenue stacking (FCR-N, FCR-D, mFRR, arbitrage) with Svk endurance rules: 4.15 M€/yr at 2025 prices, 1.82 M€/yr at 2026, 0.96 M€/yr in the saturated case. Base NPV ≈ 7.2 M€, IRR 34 %. The case is highly price-sensitive.
  • AI-training swings (12 MW, 6 s): smoothing needs ≈ 11.4 MW but < 0.5 MWh. The 0.1–2 Hz ripple needs a response of ≲ 10 ms, from a supercap or hybrid, UPS-level smoothing or a GPU power floor.
This is a fictional case built from public standards and assumed parameters marked [ASSUMED] / (A). No employer, client or vendor data is used.

Context: storage layers

FIG-004S v0.1, CONCEPT / DRAFT — requires review by a licensed electrical engineer. Numbers are taken unchanged from DB-001 Rev P2 and CALC-004; rack BBU and capacitor values come from the OCP spec and are not part of the Project 01 base design.

  1. Each layer covers one time band. Rack capacitors ride through up to 20 ms, the UPS covers 5 to 8.1 min (rated load at end of life; N-1 load), the campus BESS 27 min, and the gensets 24 h of fuel. Sources: OCP Mt. Diablo v1.0.0 [Verified (KB 2026-10-26)]; DB-001 P2; CALC-004.
  2. The 10 MW / 20 MWh BESS works in the band from 150 ms to hours. It is not counted in redundancy and does not replace the UPS or the gensets (CALC-004 §4).
  3. AI-training ripple at 0.1–2 Hz needs a response of about 10 ms or faster. A 150 ms BESS still leaves 5.6 MW peak-to-peak, so the fast band needs capacitors or a supercap. A 23 kWh supercap per 10 MW brings it down to 0.79 MW (CALC-004 model).
  4. The 3 MW/min ramp limit (A) is a power problem, not an energy one: about 11.4 MW but only 0.46 MWh (CALC-004).
  5. The 10–15 s gap between grid loss and the gensets is bridged by the UPS behind the open-transition ATS, not by the BESS at 20 kV (CALC-004; DB-001 IL-3).
  6. Uptime sets at least 12 h of fuel at N for all Tiers; this campus holds 24 h. Source: Uptime Institute Journal [Verified, opened 2026-10-29].
  7. Rack BBUs (45–90 s at 100 % load) would only come in with an 800 VDC rack design. See Project 05 and the OCP Mt. Diablo spec [Verified (KB 2026-10-26)].
